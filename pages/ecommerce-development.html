Ecommerce Development

Plan an online store around how shoppers find products, check delivery options and complete an order.

Choose a platform around your workflow

Shopify development can suit a team that wants a hosted storefront. WordPress and WooCommerce are worth considering if your store needs to sit alongside an existing content site. Discuss who will maintain the store, edit the catalog and handle orders before choosing.

Plan the catalog and checkout

Start with one representative product from each category. List its variants, prices, delivery options and stock rules. That gives us enough detail to map collections and test what shoppers see from product page to checkout.

Prepare an existing store for a move

Bring your current store URL, product export and important pages. We can scope redirects and check how orders, inventory and shipping are handled before a launch date is set.

What to include in a store brief

Product sample
Send a few products with variants, images, prices and stock rules.
Shipping and payment
List the countries you serve, delivery costs and payment providers you can use.
Launch checks
Include a mobile test order, order notification and a review of any URLs that will change.

Plan how customers will find products

A catalog that works for ten products may break down at a hundred. Group products by how customers shop, such as type, size or use, and decide which attributes should become filters. Product pages need the details a buyer asks about before checkout: price, variant options, stock, delivery and returns.

For a clothing store, create a sample product with two sizes and two colors, one unavailable combination and a size guide. Test browsing from collection to product page, then try adding only an available variant to the basket. Use the same test on a narrow mobile screen.

Set up checkout around your markets

Write down where you sell and whether you ship nationwide, offer local collection or support international delivery. Decide how the site should explain excluded destinations, unexpected shipping fees and the expected delivery window. Confirm the payment options your business can actually activate before designing the final checkout experience.

Scope the test orders you want to see before launch, such as a successful order, a failed payment, a discount code and a delivery address outside your service area. The person responsible for fulfillment should also check order notifications and the stock update.

Plan tracking and store handover

Separate a product view and a checkout start from a completed order. This makes it easier to see where shoppers drop off, especially if you’ll later run paid ads to product or collection pages. When moving stores, list old product and collection links so useful URLs have a destination.

In the handover, walk through editing a price, changing stock, fulfilling an order and handling a return. Catalog size, data quality and integrations with inventory, couriers or reporting shape the quote and timeline.

FAQ

Do I need all my products ready before contacting you?

A representative sample is enough to start. Variants, images, shipping rules and your target markets help us scope the full catalog.

Can I keep my existing payment provider?

Compatibility depends on your platform, business location and provider account. We can review your requirements while scoping the project.
